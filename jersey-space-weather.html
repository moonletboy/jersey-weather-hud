AURORA OVAL · JERSEY

VISIBILITY CHECK

The aurora forms an oval-shaped ring around the magnetic pole. As geomagnetic activity (Kp) rises, that ring expands further from the pole and can push south enough to reach Jersey's latitude — 49.18°N. Because Jersey sits much further south than typical aurora-viewing latitudes, this only happens during severe-to-extreme geomagnetic storms (Kp 7–9). The estimate below is a rough guide based on Kp, not a precise real-time position.

BEST VANTAGE POINTS

JERSEY

Aurora appears low on the northern horizon, so the best spots are elevated, north-facing, and away from light pollution. These north-coast headlands give the clearest, darkest sightlines out over the sea — tap a marker for details.

GROSNEZ POINT
Jersey's northwest tip — wide open sea horizon, minimal light pollution, elevated castle ruins headland.
PLEMONT POINT
High north-coast headland with an unobstructed view out to sea, well away from St Helier's glow.
SOREL POINT
The highest point on the island's north coast — a popular local stargazing spot for a reason.
BONNE NUIT / RONEZ
Elevated headland with a clear north-facing sea view and dark surroundings.
BOULEY BAY
Steep north-coast bay with an open horizon; the viewpoint car park at the top gives good elevation.
